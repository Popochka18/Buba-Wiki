## Role in the Council

Unlike Bomilcar, who openly challenges Hannibal, and Adherbal, who proposes a concrete plan of retreat, Hanno takes a wait-and-see stance at the council. When Hannibal voices the idea of negotiating with Mateusz, it is Hanno — with a glint in his small eyes — who first asks what exactly the enemy wants, clearly weighing the situation from the point of view of a possible deal rather than principle.

When Hannibal delivers his final speech about the meaning of war and his weariness of serving the empire, Hanno's reaction is described as "calculation" — in contrast to Mago's pain, Adherbal's bewilderment, or Bomilcar's hatred.

## Hannibal's Execution

Hanno is among the officers who come to Hannibal's tent before dawn with swords in hand. At the moment of the killing, while Bomilcar strikes the blows and Adherbal and Mago freeze in horror and pain, Hanno once again reacts with "calculation" — already thinking of how to turn what is happening to his own advantage. No further actions or lines from Hanno are given in this scene — his role is limited to that of a silent observer profiting from another man's decision.

## Execution

After the destruction of the [[Хель Ливийская|Libyan Hel]], he was captured and publicly hanged in Krakow together with [[Бомилькар|Bomilcar]] and two other officers.
