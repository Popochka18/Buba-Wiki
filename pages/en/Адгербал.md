## Role in the Council

When Hannibal's army, worn down by Mateusz's guerrilla war, dwindles from twenty to fifteen thousand men, and word arrives of the fall of Kiev to Vladimir's army, Adherbal proposes the most pragmatic of solutions at the council — a retreat south through the mountains, a regrouping at Hadrumetum, and the forming of a new army come spring. Asked about the fate of the lost lands, he answers calmly: "Lost. For now, at least. But the army will be saved. That is what matters."

When Hannibal instead proposes negotiating with Mateusz and granting Polonia its freedom, Adherbal reacts not with anger, as [[Бомилькар|Bomilcar]] does, but with bewilderment — failing to understand why the commander would refuse the obviously safer option.

## Hannibal's Death

Adherbal is among those who come to [[Ганнибал Барка|Hannibal]]'s tent before dawn with swords — ostensibly to "arrest him for treason." Unlike Bomilcar, who deals the fatal blows, and [[Ханно|Hanno]], who is already calculating the gains of a change in power, Adherbal during the execution looks like a man seized with horror, one who has realized that they have crossed a line.

After [[Ганнибал Барка|Hannibal]]'s death, the new commander [[Бомилькар|Bomilcar]] orders the army to retreat — the very plan that Adherbal had originally proposed and that [[Ганнибал Барка|Hannibal]] had opposed. The irony of fate: Adherbal's plan is carried out after all, but no longer as a reasoned decision — rather as the consequence of a murder.

## Fate After the War

He likely died in one of the skirmishes or was executed by the rebels. The report to Mateusz stated that all the officers of the [[Хель Ливийская|Libyan Hel]] had been executed.
