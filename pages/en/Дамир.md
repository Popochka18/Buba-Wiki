Damir is a mercenary, one of the three founders of "the firm" that specialized in solving the problems of the nobility, alongside [[Максим|Maxim]] and [[Фурио|Furio]]. The most level-headed of the trio and the group's de facto leader. He died in [[Масиаф|Masyaf]], having betrayed the interests of his employer and his own partners to save the runaway elf-woman [[Ульрика|Ulrika]] and her protector [[Аврам|Avram]] — and was killed for it by his own friends.

## Origins (Book 5)

### Chapter 1: "Aaron Has Returned"

At fifteen, Damir finds himself on the verge of execution — a Carthaginian is preparing to hang him for an unnamed crime. At that moment [[Аарон|Aaron]] appears, an Ahentian merchant, and buys Damir from the Carthaginian, saving his life. This bargain lays the foundation of a long and ambiguous bond between them: years later, in [[Масиаф|Masyaf]], it is [[Аарон|Aaron]] — now the owner of a teahouse and shops across a whole district — who recognizes Damir with a familiar "Long time, no see" and remarks, "You've changed, Damigh." That same bond leads Damir to his final, fatal job.

### Chapter 3: "Polonia"

At twenty, Damir finds himself in [[Краков|Krakow]], where he plays cards with a Carthaginian officer. After losing, the officer tries to kill him — but Damir single-handedly destroys his entire detachment and is forced to flee pursuit. This episode shows what will become Damir's hallmark in adult life: a calm that turns into a swift and deadly reaction when it comes to a fight, and a readiness to vanish without a trace when circumstances demand it.

## "The Firm" and Ten Years on the Road

By the time of the events in [[Масиаф|Masyaf]], Damir, [[Максим|Maxim]] and [[Фурио|Furio]] are long-time partners who have worked together for more than ten years. Their "firm" solves the problems of the nobility — usually problems "that had names, families and a habit of getting in the way" — allowing their clients to keep their hands clean. Of the trio, Damir is the most composed: he allows himself no outbursts, though, as the narrator notes, he had "more than enough" reasons for them. It is he who usually defuses conflicts between the impulsive [[Максим|Maxim]] (a Polonian noble, unkempt and quick to lose his temper) and the coldly ironic [[Фурио|Furio]] (a Tilian who wears a braid as a trophy for an Estalian general he killed).

## The Last Job in Masyaf

In [[Масиаф|Masyaf]] — once the centre of the world's slave trade, now "a rotting tooth in the mouth of a dying empire" — the trio is hired by [[Аарон|Aaron]] on behalf of [[Рашид|Rashid]], the younger son of the caliph of [[Сардония|Sardonia]], who exiled his elder brother [[Амир|Amir]]. A slave of Rashid's has fled — a forest elf, [[Ульрика|Ulrika]] — and he promises ten sacks of gold for her return, alive and unharmed.

![Наёмник_сидит_в_таверне_202606150035.jpeg](Наёмник_сидит_в_таверне_202606150035.jpeg)

Damir conducts the investigation methodically: the teahouse of the lower district, the Temple of the Nameless One (a refuge for those "whom no one is looking for, or whom too many are looking for"), a blind soothsayer who points the way to the old wharf where the smugglers of fugitives hide.

### The Meeting with Ulrika and Avram

At the old wharf the trio finds [[Ульрика|Ulrika]] under the protection of [[Аврам|Avram]] — Rashid's former personal executioner, who fled together with her. Ulrika recounts that for three years she was Rashid's plaything, that he killed her friends before her eyes as a "lesson," and that for escape he flays people alive. Avram explains his deed simply: "I grew tired of killing those who did not deserve death. Tired of being what he made me. And tired of seeing what he does to her."

### The Choice and the Betrayal

Damir decides to let them both go — to lie to the client that the trail went cold at the sea. [[Максим|Maxim]], seized by greed at the thought of the lost ten sacks of gold, demands they continue the hunt; when Damir refuses, Maxim lunges at him with his sword. Damir effortlessly takes both [[Максим|Maxim]] and [[Фурио|Furio]] out of the fight — with a blow of the hilt and a fist — and leaves together with [[Ульрика|Ulrika]] and [[Аврам|Avram]] on three bought horses, planning to slip north through [[Гелдор|Geldor]] toward the Great Steppes, far from Rashid's men.

That same night [[Максим|Maxim]] hires fifteen mercenaries and gives chase. In the clash that breaks out on the road, Damir cuts three fingers from [[Максим|Maxim]]'s hand in the fight; [[Аврам|Avram]] dies, run through by a blade from behind. In the heat of the fight with Maxim, [[Фурио|Furio]] comes up from behind and, without warning, runs Damir through with his sword — "nothing personal, just business" — after which Maxim beheads his already dying friend. [[Ульрика|Ulrika]] manages to escape north — a path that will later lead her to [[Муаммар|Muammar]] and on to [[Владимир|Vladimir]].

## Personality

Damir is described as the most "sane" of the trio — not because he is free of inner demons, but because he learned to keep them "neat and secure, like a sharpened knife in its sheath." Decades of work in which people were merchandise and conscience a luxury did not kill in him the ability to see others as people rather than targets. His decisive words — "I am tired of being what this work makes me" — become not only the cause of his death but, in essence, a brief statement of the theme that runs through the stories of many of the work's characters: the possibility (and the price) of refusing violence that has become habitual.

For all that, Damir is not idealized: cutting off three of [[Максим|Maxim]]'s fingers is the reflex of a professional fighter, not an act of mercy, and his decision costs the life not only of himself but also of [[Аврам|Avram]].

## Legacy

Damir's death breaks [[Максим|Maxim]], who three months later is still drowning his guilt in cheap liquor at the tavern "The Lame Rooster" in [[Белосток|Bialystok]] — the very place where [[Янек|Janek]]'s story begins. [[Фурио|Furio]] calls Damir's death his own choice ("he got what he wanted") and goes on working as if nothing had happened. The chief result of Damir's deed, however, is the rescued [[Ульрика|Ulrika]], who thanks to his decision lives to meet [[Муаммар|Muammar]] and becomes one of [[Владимир|Vladimir]]'s companions in the north.
