## History

### Formation in Syrtis (prequel, Book 5, Chapter III "The Deceived Traitor")

The squad's origins go back to Khalid's exile from Syrtis — deceived and branded a traitor, he flees the city, managing to carry off money from the city treasury. With these funds he begins to gather bandits and fugitives around him — men with no home and no past, ready to follow whoever gives them a purpose. Thus the core of the future squad is born.

### Revenge in Syrtis (prequel, Book 5, Chapter VII "Revenge")

When General Mago Barcid arrives in Syrtis, Khalid seizes it as a chance for retribution — and kills him. This murder is the first step on Khalifa Haftar's path to a reputation as the executioner of Carthage, and at the same time a symbolic prelude to the squad's final meeting with another member of the House of the Barcids — Hannibal Barca — years later, in the Gorge of the Red Wind.

### Operations of the "Sand Wolves"

By the time Amir al-Faris joins the squad, the band numbers forty-three men and operates methodically and shrewdly — raids on caravans are planned with precision, with the size of the guard, the routes and the weaponry all known in advance. Their base is the oasis of Zar-Kala — a patch of green amid the sands, marked on no Carthaginian map.

### The Gorge of the Red Wind

The squad's last and greatest operation is an ambush on Hannibal Barca's army returning from the ruined Al-Qasar. The plan rests on dividing their forces: the archers (12 men) take the eastern slope, the swordsmen (20 men, including Amir) wait in the narrow pass, and the rest prepare the rockfall that is to cut the army's vanguard (around 50–60 men, including the personal guard and Hannibal himself) off from the main column.

The ambush works, but the Carthaginians quickly regroup and counterattack. The squad is almost entirely wiped out. Khalid himself challenges Hannibal Barca to single combat — and loses it, dying in Amir's arms. Before his death he reveals his true name (Khalifa Haftar) and hands Amir a map to hidden treasure, asking him to use it not for himself but for the war against Carthage — as atonement. Hannibal, learning whom he had fought, lets the two survivors — Amir and Farid — go free.
